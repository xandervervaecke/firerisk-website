## Goals

WP4 develops a high-resolution fire spread model tailored to the environmental conditions of Flanders. The model quantifies the impact of stakeholder-informed scenarios for land management and design, and so supports decision-making.

It builds on the presence of WUI (WP1), fuel and fire weather data (WP2) and land management impacts and scenarios (WP3), complemented with model-specific data. Both process-based models (such as FlamMap and SPITFIRE) and deep learning approaches are considered.

## Tasks

### Task 4.1: Literature review and model selection

Available wildfire propagation and spread models are listed and ranked on spatio-temporal scale, representativeness of their training data, predictive performance and ecological interpretability, to select the most appropriate model for Flanders.

### Task 4.2: Model training

The selected model is calibrated and validated with the WP2 datasets, model-specific variables such as midflame wind speed and topography, and historical fire occurrences, for example Kalmthoutse Heide (2011), Brecht (2021) and Mechelse Heide (2025). A European fire events dataset with fire progression geometries (2012–2023), generated within the COST Action NERO, provides further candidate validation sites. Error propagation is performed, and the model is continuously improved as updated inputs and stakeholder feedback become available.

### Task 4.3: Management scenario calculation

The management scenarios from WP3 are used to quantify the effect of land management and design on fire spread, simulating fire-prone landscapes with and without targeted prevention measures. The results feed the decision support tool for fire mitigation management.
