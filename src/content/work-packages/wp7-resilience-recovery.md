## Goals

Some ecosystems depend on fire as part of their natural cycle, but the increasing intensity and frequency of wildfires under climate change challenge the resilience of many systems. Post-fire management must therefore evolve from a purely reactive emergency response into a proactive, integrated and science-based strategy.

WP7 combines in situ data with remote sensing to compare the long-term impacts of uncontrolled wildfires with those of planned restoration fires. The aim is a stronger scientific basis for designing and managing fire-resilient landscapes that protect people, ecosystem services, cultural values and economies, and that are adaptable to local conditions.

## Tasks

### Task 7.1: Key indicators for post-fire resilience and recovery

A set of ecosystem health indicators that are especially sensitive to fire (such as soil composition, vegetation structure and cover, and mycorrhizal diversity) is selected from the literature and expert and stakeholder consultation. Reference conditions are derived from INBO measurement networks and existing monitoring plots.

### Task 7.2: Post-fire resilience monitoring plots

Forty to fifty monitoring plots are established in burned areas, paired with unburned control plots and sites designated for prescribed burning, in the same ten study sites used in WP2 and WP3. Field assessments, lab analyses and eDNA analyses of soil mycorrhizal communities compare the effects of prescribed and uncontrolled fires along gradients of fire intensity and time since fire.

### Task 7.3: Resilience and restoration analysis

Generalised mixed-effects models assess the impact of wildfire occurrence and severity on each indicator. The open-source PEOPLE-ER Spectral Recovery tool is used to visualise recovery trajectories from satellite time series, linked to pre- and post-fire management and ecosystem characteristics.

### Task 7.4: Decision support tool for fire-resilient management and restoration

A spatially explicit proof of concept shows how management options (prescribed burning, thinning, restoration planting) affect fire risk and ecosystem resilience. Land managers can compare alternatives and trade-offs such as burned area, habitat and smoke. The tool is co-created with the end-user group of land and nature managers, with documentation in their preferred language.
