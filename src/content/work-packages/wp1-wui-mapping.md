## Goals

Where houses lie close to, or intermixed with, wildland fuels, both the probability of ignition and the potential impact of a wildfire increase. This proximity is the usual way to describe **wildfire exposure**. To assess and manage wildfire risk, spatially explicit information about these wildland–urban interfaces (WUIs) is essential.

WUIs are locations *where settlements are adjacent to or interspersed with wildland vegetation*. At a first, coarse level they are divided into **interface** and **intermix** WUI. Identifying them requires a spatially explicit set of rules adapted to the local context.

WP1 will generate the first spatial assessment of WUIs in Flanders, with in-depth structural information on the WUIs and the spread of built-up areas, combined with the critical infrastructure and building assets present.

## Tasks

### Task 1.1: WUI definition

Locally specific WUI definitions will be developed, benchmarked against existing definitions and extended with local variables related to critical infrastructure. Both intermix and interface WUI types are considered. Threshold values for the density of anthropogenic assets (including buildings) and the proximity of wildland fuels will be based on the literature and discussed and validated with stakeholders such as emergency planners and fire districts (WP9).

### Task 1.2: Spatial assessment

High-resolution input datasets describing the anthropogenic assets identified in Task 1.1 will be combined with existing geodatasets of wildland fuel types, produced by the BELSPO-funded FuelFusion project. Spatial overlays follow the definitions from Task 1.1. WUI characteristics (total area and the fractions of intermix and interface) will be benchmarked against low-resolution European estimates.
