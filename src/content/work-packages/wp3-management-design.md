## Goals

WP3 explores how land management and landscape characteristics influence the fundamental determinants of wildfire behaviour (fire intensity, spread and overall severity) across ten diverse study areas. It assesses the effects of current practices, including hydrological interventions, vegetation management, recreational use and policy measures, on landscape composition at parcel and landscape scale.

Building on WP1 and WP2, it develops management scenarios for the fire spread model of WP4, co-created with stakeholders through WP9. Fire-smart and functional approaches are central.

## Tasks

### Task 3.1: Multidimensional characterisation

The ten fire-prone study sites are described along four dimensions: ecosystems, fuel types and loads, abiotic factors such as soil and ecohydrology, management practices including protection status and fire prevention, and human use such as recreation, military use and WUI.

### Task 3.2: Data collection

Together with local managers, existing geospatial data on ecosystems, the abiotic environment and (historic) land management are collected and harmonised for each study site. Fuel load, LFMC and microclimate data come from WP2.

### Task 3.3: Assessing impacts

Statistical models first examine the relationships between land management and landscape characteristics. Machine learning models such as XGBoost and random forest then translate landscape characteristics into fuel type, fuel load, LFMC and microclimate variables. Results are discussed with the end-user group of land and nature managers.

### Task 3.4: Management scenario development

From the literature and the outcomes of Task 3.3, common fire mitigation measures (such as prescribed burning, firebreaks, fuel removal and access restrictions) are compiled into a virtual dataset of management scenarios. The trained models infer the associated fuel and microclimate variables, which feed the scenario analysis in WP4.
