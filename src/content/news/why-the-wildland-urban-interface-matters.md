The wildland–urban interface, or WUI, is where settlements are adjacent to or interspersed with wildland vegetation. Where houses and wildland fuels meet, both the probability of ignition and the potential impact of a wildfire on homes, infrastructure and people increase.

## A surprisingly large interface

Belgium is not usually thought of as a wildfire country. Yet the WUI is estimated to cover **14.8% of Belgian territory**: substantially more than in fire-prone countries such as Greece (6.9%) or Spain (4.5%). In a densely built landscape where heathland and forest lie close to towns and villages, many people live near vegetation that can burn.

## Why a local map is needed

There is no harmonised definition of the WUI, and pan-European systems work at coarse spatial resolution. FireRisk therefore develops locally specific WUI definitions with emergency planners and fire districts, and maps the WUI across Flanders at 10 × 10 m resolution, including critical infrastructure and building assets.
